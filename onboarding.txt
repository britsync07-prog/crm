43. FINAL UX PRINCIPLE

The internal user should feel like they are working with an AI operations assistant.

Not:

"Complete this 30-field onboarding form."

Instead:

AI: ABC Ltd is ready for onboarding. I already have most information. I need five details before I can prepare everything.

Then:

AI: I've prepared 9 actions. Three require your approval.

Then:

AI: The client has completed onboarding except for API access. I've prepared a request for your approval.

Then:

AI: Everything required is complete. Client is ready for activation.

The human's job becomes:

Answer → Review → Approve → Handle Exceptions

The AI's job becomes:

Analyse → Prepare → Generate → Monitor → Recommend → Coordinate

The CRM's job becomes:

Execute → Track → Store → Audit

44. SUCCESS CRITERIA

The system will be considered successful when a normal client can go from:

Won Deal → Active Client

with minimal human intervention.

Target:

Internal setup: under 2 minutes
Client onboarding: 3–10 minutes for a normal client
No duplicate questions
No unnecessary forms
All required documents generated automatically
All sensitive external actions require approval
Digital signing works from mobile
Invoice integrates automatically
Follow-ups are automatically prepared
Every action has an audit record
Exceptions are clearly surfaced
New services can be onboarded by creating a new template rather than rebuilding the system
MOST IMPORTANT RULE

Do not build a collection of disconnected onboarding features.

Build a single AI-driven Onboarding Engine where:

Client + Service + Template + Workflow + AI Actions + Human Approval + Client Responses + Documents + Signatures + Invoice + Communication + Audit Trail

all belong to one OnboardingInstance.

The system should always be able to answer:

Who is this client?

What did they buy?

What has already been completed?

What is still required?

Who needs to act?

What should happen next?

Can AI do it, or does it require human approval?

What evidence do we have that it happened?

Build the system around those questions.
